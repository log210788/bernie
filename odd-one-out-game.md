# 🎯 The "Odd One Out" Vocabulary Game (El Intruso)
### Field Vocabulary & Tactical Thinking Game for Bernie 🇦🇷

---

## 🇪🇸 Explicación del Juego en Español (Para Bernie)

### ¿De qué se trata el juego "Odd One Out"?
**"Odd One Out"** (literalmente: *"el que no encaja"* o *"el intruso"*) es una dinámica ágil y entretenida para poner a prueba tu vocabulario, tu rapidez mental y tu capacidad de argumentar en inglés.

En cada ronda te voy a dar **un grupo de 4 palabras**.  
Tres de ellas tienen algo muy claro en común (pertenecen a la misma categoría operativa, son herramientas del mismo tipo, o se usan en la misma fase de una emergencia).  
**Una sola palabra NO pertenece al grupo: esa es "the odd one out" (el intruso).**

### Tu misión en la clase:
1. **Identificar cuál es la palabra intrusa.**
2. **Explicar en voz alta POR QUÉ no encaja en inglés.**  
   *(No te preocupes por hablar perfecto; lo importante es justificar tu decisión con oraciones claras y directas).*

---

## 🗣️ Frases Útiles para Responder en Inglés (Cheat Sheet de Respuesta)

Usá estas fórmulas para armar tu justificación:

* **Para señalar al intruso:**
  * *"**[Palabra]** is the odd one out because..."* *(...es la intrusa porque...)*
  * *"The odd one out is **[Palabra]**."* *(La intrusa es...)*

* **Para agrupar a las otras tres:**
  * *"The other three are all **[categoría: tools / hazards / triage colors / commands]**."*  
    *(Las otras tres son todas...)*
  * *"A, B, and C are used for **[acción]**, but X is for **[otra acción]**."*  
    *(A, B y C se usan para..., pero X es para...)*

* **Usando "Should" y "Would" (de la Lección 5):**
  * *"In this situation we **should** use these three, but we **wouldn't** use [Palabra]."*  
    *(En esta situación deberíamos usar estas tres, pero no usaríamos...)*
  * *"You **should** be careful with these hazards, but a safe zone is secure."*

## 🎮 Game Cards: 8 Rondas Temáticas de Vocabulario de Campo

---

### Round 1: Heavy Rescue Equipment (Equipo de Rescate Pesado)
* **Words:**
  1. `Hydraulic spreaders` (Expansores hidráulicos)
  2. `Rotary rescue saw` (Motosierra rotativa de rescate)
  3. `Timber shoring` (Apuntalamiento de madera)
  4. `Tea towel` (Repasador / Trapo de cocina)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Tea towel`**
* **Why?** Hydraulic spreaders, rotary saws, and timber shoring are heavy USAR rescue tools for collapsed structures. A tea towel is household kitchen linen from our spatial practice.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Tea towel** is the odd one out. The other three are heavy rescue tools for collapsed buildings, but a tea towel is for the kitchen."*

---

### Round 2: Medical Triage Categories (Triaje Médico)
* **Words:**
  1. `Critical (Red)` (Crítico / Rojo)
  2. `Delayed / Serious (Yellow)` (Demorado / Amarillo)
  3. `Impassable` (Intransitable / Bloqueado)
  4. `Walking wounded (Green)` (Herido leve / Verde)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Impassable`**
* **Why?** Red, Yellow, and Green are patient medical triage categories. *Impassable* describes road access conditions, not human casualties.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Impassable** is the odd one out. Red, Yellow, and Green are triage priorities for patients, but impassable describes a road."*

---

### Round 3: Ground-Zero Hazards (Peligros en la Zona de Impacto)
* **Words:**
  1. `Confirmed gas leak` (Fuga de gas confirmada)
  2. `Live power lines` (Cables eléctricos con corriente)
  3. `Secondary collapse risk` (Riesgo de colapso secundario)
  4. `Safe zone` (Zona segura)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Safe zone`**
* **Why?** Gas leaks, live wires, and secondary collapse are lethal hazards found in the right quadrant of a search marking. A safe zone is a secure, open area free from danger.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Safe zone** is the odd one out. The other three are dangerous hazards, but a safe zone is where people are protected."*

---

### Round 4: Search & Detection Methods (Métodos de Búsqueda)
* **Words:**
  1. `Acoustic listening device` (Geófono / Dispositivo de escucha acústica)
  2. `K9 search dog unit` (Binomio canino de rastreo)
  3. `Snake camera` (Cámara de búsqueda con videoscopio)
  4. `Spine board` (Tabla espinal de inmovilización)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Spine board`**
* **Why?** Acoustic devices, K9 dogs, and snake cameras are used to *locate* trapped victims under rubble. A spine board is used for *medical immobilization and transport* after the victim is extricated.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Spine board** is the odd one out. Dogs, cameras, and acoustic devices are used to find victims, but a spine board is for medical transport."*

---

### Round 5: Road Access & Logistics (Condiciones de Caminos)
* **Words:**
  1. `Collapsed bridge` (Puente derrumbado)
  2. `Mudslide blockage` (Corte por deslave / alud)
  3. `Passable for 4x4 only` (Transitable solo para 4x4)
  4. `Frying pan` (Sartén)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Frying pan`**
* **Why?** Collapsed bridges, mudslides, and 4x4 passability are road logistics terms. A frying pan is kitchen cookware.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Frying pan** is the odd one out. The other three describe route access for supply convoys, but a frying pan is for cooking."*

---

### Round 6: Urgent Imperatives to Crowds (Comandos de Voz a la Multitud)
* **Words:**
  1. `Step back!` (¡Atrás! / ¡Paso atrás!)
  2. `Keep clear of the building!` (¡Despejen el edificio!)
  3. `Unconscious` (Inconsciente)
  4. `Clear the area immediately!` (¡Desalojen la zona de inmediato!)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`Unconscious`**
* **Why?** *Step back!*, *Keep clear!*, and *Clear the area!* are loud imperative commands given to bystanders. *Unconscious* is an adjective describing a patient's medical state.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**Unconscious** is the odd one out. The other three are direct verbal commands for crowd safety, but unconscious is a medical condition."*

---

### Round 7: Spatial Prepositions in the Field (Preposiciones Espaciales)
* **Words:**
  1. `Under the rubble` (Bajo los escombros)
  2. `In the void space` (En el hueco de supervivencia)
  3. `On the top shelf` (En el estante superior)
  4. `Between the concrete slabs` (Entre las losas de hormigón)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`On the top shelf`**
* **Why?** *Under the rubble*, *in the void space*, and *between the concrete slabs* describe places where survivors or tools are located in a disaster collapse. *On the top shelf* describes domestic storage in a room or closet.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**On the top shelf** is the odd one out. The other three are disaster collapse locations, while top shelf is for a house or closet."*

---

### Round 8: Modal Verbs & Field Decisions (Should vs Would - Lección 5)
* **Words:**
  1. `We should isolate the gas.` (Deberíamos cortar el gas - Protocolo)
  2. `Responders should wear helmets.` (Los rescatistas deberían usar casco - Regla)
  3. `Triage teams should tag patients.` (Los equipos de triaje deberían clasificar pacientes - Norma)
  4. `I would eat grandma's soup.` (Yo tomaría la sopa de la abuela - Elección personal)
* **The Odd One Out:** **`I would eat grandma's soup.`**
* **Why?** The first three are mandatory operational protocols using `should`. The last one is a personal preference using `would`.
* **Model Answer for Bernie:**  
  > *"**I would eat grandma's soup** is the odd one out! The other three are official safety protocols with 'should', but the soup is personal comfort!"*

## 💡 Quick Tips for Louie when Running This in Class:

2. **Encourage full sentences:** Don't let her just say *"Tea towel"*. Prompt: *"Why, Bernie? What do the other three have in common?"*
3. **Award points or celebratory praise:** When she explains the reason, praise her operational logic: *"Spot on! Exactly, those are USAR tools, whereas that's domestic."*
