> **💡 Tutor Tip for Louie (Consejo pedagógico para Lewis):**  
> 1. **Check-in on her recovery:** Bernie was under the weather in the last session. Start with a warm 2-minute catch-up asking how she is feeling, if the chicken soup helped, and if she got some well-deserved rest.
> 2. **Bridge from the "Unpacking" game to ground zero:** In the last class, she mastered spatial prepositions (*"under the sink", "on the top shelf", "in the drawer"*). Quickly show her that these exact prepositions describe disaster sites: *"The victim is **under** the concrete slab"*, *"The med kit is **in** the compartment"*, *"The crack is **above** the lintel"*.
> 3. **The Core Linguistic Target ("Should" vs "Would"):**
>    - Many Spanish speakers confuse **"Should"** (*debería / deberíamos* = protocol, duty, best practice) with **"Would"** (*haría / harías* = hypothetical personal judgment, instinct in an unforeseen dilemma).
>    - In disaster relief, this distinction is vital:
>      - *"What **should** we do?"* = What does the SOP / INSARAG safety rule say we must do?
>      - *"What **would** you do if...?"* = What is your tactical choice when the protocol is impossible or conditions change unexpectedly?
> 4. **Pronunciation Alert (Silent 'L'):**
>    - Spanish speakers instinctively want to pronounce the 'L' in *should* and *would*. Drill right away:  
>      * **Should** = `/ʃʊd/` (*SHUD* — rhymes with *good/could*). The 'L' is completely silent!  
>      * **Would** = `/wʊd/` (*WUD* — sounds identical to *wood*). The 'L' is completely silent!  
>      * **Shouldn't** = `/ˈʃʊd.ənt/` (*SHUD-nt*).  
>      * **Wouldn't** = `/ˈwʊd.ənt/` (*WUD-nt*).

## Part 2: Core Grammar & Functional Language: "Should" vs "Would" (15 mins)

Explain the contrast simply in English, supporting with Argentine Spanish references so Bernie's tactical brain connects instantly:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   📋 SHOULD  vs  💭 WOULD                              │
├──────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 📋 SHOULD  (Protocol / Obligation)│ 💭 WOULD  (Hypothetical / Instinct) │
│                                  │                                     │
│ "What SHOULD we do?"             │ "What WOULD you do if...?"          │
│ (= ¿Qué deberíamos hacer?)       │ (= ¿Qué harías vos si...?)          │
│                                  │                                     │
│ Used for: Standard Operating     │ Used for: Scenarios, unexpected     │
│ Procedures (SOP), rules, safety  │ dilemmas, personal field judgment,  │
│ recommendations, official steps. │ conditional situations.             │
│                                  │                                     │
│ Formula: Subject + SHOULD + Verb │ Formula: Subject + WOULD + Verb     │
│ "We should isolate the gas."     │ "I would order a tactical retreat." │
└──────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Pronunciation & Phonetics Table:
*(Remind Bernie: No 'L' sound!)*

| Word / Phrase | Meaning | Pronunciación aprox. | Common Error to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Should** *(modal)* | Debería / Deberíamos | *SHUD* (/ʃʊd/) | ❌ Do NOT say *"shold"* or *"shuld"*. Rhymes with *good*. |
| **Should not / Shouldn't** | No debería / No deberíamos | *SHUD-nt* (/ˈʃʊd.nt/) | Clear dental *t* at the end. |
| **Would** *(modal)* | Haría / Harías / Haríamos | *WUD* (/wʊd/) | ❌ Do NOT pronounce the 'L'. Sounds exactly like *wood* (madera). |
| **Would not / Wouldn't** | No haría / No haríamos | *WUD-nt* (/ˈwʊd.nt/) | Contraction used in natural speech. |
| **"What should we do?"** | ¿Qué deberíamos hacer? | *WOT SHUD wi DUU?* | The go-to question when consulting standard protocols. |
| **"What would you do?"** | ¿Qué harías vos? | *WOT WUD yu DUU?* | The go-to question when asking for tactical advice in a dilemma. |

## Part 3: Operational Decision Formulas (15 mins)

Teach Bernie these 6 high-frequency field formulas used in UN / INSARAG incident command meetings:

### 1. 🛡️ Stating Official Field Protocols (`SHOULD`):
* **"We should establish a 50-meter safety perimeter immediately."**  
  *(Deberíamos establecer un perímetro de seguridad de 50 metros de inmediato.)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *wi SHUD e-STÁ-blish a FÍF-ti MÍI-ter SÉIF-ti pe-RÍ-mi-ter i-MÍ-diat-li.*
* **"Responders should not enter without structural shoring."**  
  *(Los rescatistas no deberían ingresar sin apuntalamiento estructural.)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *ri-SPÓN-derz SHUD NOT ÉN-ter with-ÁUT STRÁK-cher-al SHÓ-ring.*
* **"We should check for live power lines before spraying water."**  
  *(Deberíamos verificar si hay cables eléctricos con corriente antes de tirar agua.)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *wi SHUD CHEK for LAIV PÁU-er lains bi-FÓR SPRÉI-ing WÓ-ter.*

### 2. ⚡ Solving Unforeseen Field Dilemmas (`WOULD`):
* **"If the main access road is impassable, I would divert the convoy to Route 4."**  
  *(Si el camino principal está intransitable, yo desviaría el convoy hacia la Ruta 4.)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *if the MEIN ÁK-ses roud iz im-PÁ-sa-bl, ai WUD dai-VÉRT the KÓN-voi tu RUUT FOR.*
* **"If the crane is delayed, what would you do?"**  
  *(Si la grúa se retrasa, ¿qué harías vos?)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *if the KREIN iz di-LÉID, WOT WUD yu DUU?*
* **"I would stabilize the victim with IV fluids and wait for heavy lifting airbags."**  
  *(Yo estabilizaría a la víctima con suero intravenoso y esperaría los cojines neumáticos.)*  
  *Pronunciation:* *ai WUD stei-bi-LÁIZ the VÍK-tim with AI-VII FLÚU-ids and WEIT for HÉ-vi LÍF-ting ÉAR-bags.*

### 3. ⚖️ Field Trade-Offs: "Would you rather...?" (Evaluación de alternativas):
* **"Would you rather wait 30 minutes for the K9 dog unit, or start acoustic listening now?"**  
  *(¿Preferirías esperar 30 minutos a los perros o empezar la búsqueda acústica ahora?)*  
* **"I would rather start acoustic search immediately to save critical minutes."**  
  *(Preferiría iniciar la búsqueda acústica de inmediato para ganar minutos críticos.)*

## Part 4: Tactical Field Scenarios: "What Should We Do?" vs "What Would You Do?" (15 mins)

Work through these 4 realistic disaster dilemmas with Bernie. Louie presents the situation, asks the prompt question, and guides Bernie to form full operational sentences.

---

### 🚨 Dilemma 1: Gas Odor & The Distraught Relative
```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SITUATION REPORT:                                                       |
| • Sector 3: A collapsed 3-story residential building.                   |
| • Search team smells a strong odor of domestic gas (confirmed leak).     |
| • An anxious father is trying to push past the police tape to dig with  |
|   his bare hands for his daughter.                                      |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
* **Question 1 (Protocol):** *"Bernie, what **should** we do about the gas leak according to standard protocol?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"We **should** turn off the gas main immediately and prohibit all open flames or power tools."*
* **Question 2 (Tactical Dilemma):** *"The father is shouting and refuses to leave. What **would** you do to calm him down?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"I **would** assign an officer to escort him to the safe zone and reassure him: 'We are locating your daughter now, but the gas is explosive. Please step back to the safe zone.'"*
* **Question 3 (Negative Should):** *"What **shouldn't** responders do right now?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"Responders **should not** use motorized rotary saws or enter the void until ventilation is confirmed."*

---

### 🚜 Dilemma 2: Critical Patient vs Heavy Rescue Delay
```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SITUATION REPORT:                                                       |
| • A 28-year-old worker is pinned beneath a 1.5-ton concrete beam.       |
| • He has been trapped for 3 hours. His leg is severely compressed.      |
| • Dispatch says the heavy crane's ETA is 45 minutes because of traffic. |
| • The paramedic warns that crush syndrome is imminent.                  |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
* **Question 1 (Protocol):** *"Bernie, what **should** the medical team prepare before the beam is lifted?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"They **should** start IV fluid resuscitation immediately to prevent cardiac arrest from crush syndrome toxins."*
* **Question 2 (Field Dilemma):** *"45 minutes is too long. If you had manual hydraulic spreaders and timber shoring on site, what **would** you do?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"I **would** start timber shoring to secure the ceiling, and I **would** try to lift the beam 5 centimeters using hydraulic spreaders."*
* **Question 3 (Hypothetical):** *"If the ceiling started cracking while lifting, what **would** you do?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"I **would** order an immediate halt: 'Stop lifting! Fall back! The ceiling is unstable!'"*

### 🌧️ Dilemma 3: Damaged Mountain Bridge & Relief Convoy
```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SITUATION REPORT:                                                       |
| • Heavy rains triggered a mudslide.                                     |
| • Route 12 has a partially collapsed bridge.                            |
| • Three supply trucks carrying drinking water and baby food are waiting.|
| • Route 12 is blocked for heavy trucks; passable for 4x4 vehicles only. |
| • Route 9 is fully paved and open, but adds 3 hours to the journey.     |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
* **Question 1 (Protocol):** *"What **should** dispatch announce on the radio regarding Route 12?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"Dispatch **should** report: 'Route 12 is impassable for heavy trucks. Passable by 4x4 only.'"*
* **Question 2 (Decision):** *"Bernie, as logistics coordinator, what **would** you do with the 3 supply trucks?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"I **would** reroute the 3 heavy trucks via Route 9, and I **would** send two 4x4 pickup trucks ahead with emergency water rations."*

### 🏗️ Dilemma 4: Acoustic Search & Secondary Aftershocks
```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SITUATION REPORT:                                                       |
| • Your search team hears faint tapping sounds from a basement void.     |
| • A 3.8 magnitude tremor (aftershock) shakes the area for 4 seconds.    |
| • The team is eager to rush in, but bricks are falling from the cornice.|
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
* **Question 1 (Protocol):** *"What **should** the safety officer do immediately after an aftershock?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"The safety officer **should** sound three horn blasts for tactical retreat and re-evaluate structural stability."*
* **Question 2 (Tactical Judgment):** *"What **would** you say to a volunteer who wants to jump inside right now?"*  
  * **Target Bernie Response:** *"I **would** stop them and command: 'Hold your position! Do not enter! Watch out for secondary collapse!'"*

## Part 5: Practical Drills & Exercises (10 mins)

### Exercise A: Should vs Would Selection
*Elegí si corresponde **SHOULD** (protocolo/norma) o **WOULD** (hipótesis/criterio personal):*

### Exercise B: Fix the Note-Taker / ASR Transcriptions!
*Como vimos en las clases pasadas, el transcriptor automático comete errores graciosos con términos de rescate y modales. Corregí estas frases:*

| Raw Note-Taker Hearing (Error) | Bernie's Forensic Correction | Correct Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. *"We **wood** call for heavy rescue."* | *"We __________ call for heavy rescue."* | Modals: wood vs would |
| 2. *"You **shood** check the gas valve."* | *"You __________ check the gas valve."* | Spelling: shood vs should |
| 3. *"We need hydraulic **breaders** and cutters."* | *"We need hydraulic __________ and cutters."* | Rescue tool: breaders vs spreaders |
| 4. *"The road is **impossible** for trucks."* | *"The road is __________ for trucks."* | Access: impossible vs impassable |
| 5. *"Watch out for falling **the breeze**!"* | *"Watch out for falling __________!"* | Hazard: the breeze vs debris |
